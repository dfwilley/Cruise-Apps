Tips for the deck

Photographing the aurora with an iPhone
  • Turn the flash and Live Photo off. Night mode turns on by itself in the dark; tap its yellow icon and slide to the longest time (often 10–30 seconds).
  • Brace the phone on the ship's rail or a bench, or use a small tripod. On a moving ship, shorter times (3–5 seconds) are sharper.
  • Tap the screen on a distant light to focus, then lower exposure a little so the green doesn't wash out.
  • Faint aurora often looks grey to the eye but green to the camera. If you see a pale "cloud" that doesn't move with the wind, take a test shot.
Cold and batteries
  • iPhone batteries drain fast below freezing. Keep the phone in an inside pocket and bring it out for shots.
  • Start the night fully charged; a small battery pack in a warm pocket helps.
  • Thin liner gloves that work on touch screens, under warmer mittens.
Reading this app
  • Kp is a 0–9 scale of how stirred up Earth's magnetic field is. Far north (Tromsø, Alta) even 1–2 can show aurora; Bergen needs about 4, Edinburgh 5.
  • Good / Fair / Poor combines aurora activity, clouds, the moon and darkness. Too early means NOAA hasn't forecast that night yet: its Kp forecast covers 3 days and its rough outlook about 4 weeks; clouds are forecast 16 days ahead.
  • Chance nearby is NOAA's live aurora map (the "OVATION" model) for the next half hour or so.
  • Port times come from a published itinerary and are approximate. Viking's daily program on board is the final word.
